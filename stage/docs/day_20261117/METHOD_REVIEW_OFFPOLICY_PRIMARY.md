# Primary-source review: learning beyond an unsuccessful recovery teacher

Date: 2026-11-17. Scope: Stage only. This is a literature and saved-evidence review, not an experiment protocol, launch authorization, implementation, or claim of algorithmic novelty. No simulator, optimizer, training or evaluation was run. Runtime contract remains one causal recurrent Actor producing the two direct continuous actions; no planner, waypoint, teacher, policy router or Q-based action selection at runtime.

## Decision

The strongest next **algorithm family to investigate** is recurrent off-policy actor–critic learning with protected prior-data replay, using RLPD as the continuous-control reference, staged successful-prefix roll-in from JSRL, and recurrent replay contracts from R2D3/R2D2 and Ni et al. This can use an unsuccessful recovery rollout as truthful reward-bearing transition data without calling its actions successful supervision. A teacher that succeeds from every new learner-induced state is **not an algorithmic prerequisite**. The missing prerequisite is useful action/outcome coverage and a trustworthy value-learning contract; off-policy RL does not manufacture reachable success or eliminate extrapolation error.

RLPD is **not newly discovered here**: it was proposed in [the 5 November reset review](../narrow_20261105/RESEARCH_RESET_20261105.md) and reconsidered in [the 16 November review](../day_20261116/t593_research/METHOD_REVIEW.md). The contribution of this note is a primary-source comparison against the present unsuccessful-recovery evidence, not a claim of exhaustive global search or a novel solution.

Before another learner, resolve the terminal-control question using existing evidence: the recent failure is a narrow miss followed by departure, while separate runs also lose an earlier obstacle-avoidance capability. Treat these as potentially different failure mechanisms. More supervised updates with a tighter historical-action penalty have already failed to establish closed-loop retention.

## Supported comparison

Ranking below is Stage-specific judgment, not a result reported by these papers. All proposals retain direct Actor actions at deployment.

| Rank / role | Primary method and evidence | Must a teacher succeed at the new recovery state? | Stage implication and principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, continuous off-policy reference | RLPD combines prior/online data, critic LayerNorm and Q ensembles; it studies both expert and suboptimal datasets. | No. Fixed prior transitions and online exploration supply rewards and Bellman targets. | Best match to continuous actions and unavailable recovery supervision. Critic error can move the Actor in a harmful direction. |
| 1, complementary data curriculum | JSRL rolls in with a useful guide, lets the learner explore the suffix, then reduces guide steps. | The guide needs useful forward progress, not successful recovery after arbitrary student drift. | Prefer an already successful causal prefix over asking the same teachers to repair the same late miss. Mixed curriculum success is not autonomous full-origin success. |
| 2, simpler deterministic baseline | DDPGfD uses demonstrations plus self-generated replay, prioritized replay and one/n-step returns. Acme TD3fD supplies a TD3 demonstration wrapper. | No teacher query at the new state. Useful demonstrations can come from other visited states. | Useful lower-complexity reference. Demonstration replay alone is neither a recovery guarantee nor a forgetting guarantee. |
| 3, complementary retention/exploration term | SIL repeats past actions only when stored return exceeds current V. | No teacher is needed. A sufficiently good past return is needed for a positive imitation update. | Fits self-success memory, but cannot supply an absent terminal success and may prefer a shaped-return near miss. |
| Recurrent design reference, not direct port | R2D3 mixes recurrent demonstration and agent replay with n-step Q-learning. | No teacher query per new state. | Valuable sequence and burn-in design. Original action-value architecture is discrete, and computational scale is far beyond Stage's bounded CPU budgets. |
| Historical contrast | DQfD combines TD with a demonstration action-margin objective and permanently retained demos. | No continuing online teacher requirement. | Evidence for retaining demonstrations, but its discrete margin loss is not a continuous steering loss to copy. |
| Prerequisite, not a standalone solver | R2D2/Ni et al.: recurrent replay, appropriate context and separate recurrent actor/critic design. | Not applicable. | Correct recurrence is needed whichever replay algorithm is chosen; burn-in alone does not prove sufficient memory. |

### RLPD and Q ensembles

RLPD does not impose BC or reset to demonstration states. Its symmetric sampling keeps equal prior/online portions instead of letting old data dominate a seeded buffer. LayerNorm is used to regularize extrapolation; ensembles support more updates per collected transition. Environment-specific ablations matter: clipping across two critics and entropy backups are not universally best, including AntMaze. Therefore copy the **controlled baseline and ablation logic**, not every locomotion setting. [Ball et al., ICML 2023, §§4–6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/ball23a/ball23a.pdf).

The author's code confirms offline_ratio=0.5 and independent online/offline sampling; its learner optimizes Actor actions using mean Q and uses target-critic subsampling. The config selects ten critics, a two-critic target subset and critic LayerNorm. These are training components: one Actor still executes directly. This implementation is feedforward SAC; a recurrent Stage adaptation is new engineering, not a verified drop-in reproduction. [Sampling code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ikostrikov/rlpd/main/train_finetuning.py), [learner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ikostrikov/rlpd/main/rlpd/agents/sac/sac_learner.py), [config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ikostrikov/rlpd/main/configs/rlpd_config.py).

REDQ's relevant contribution is the combination of high update-to-data ratio, a Q ensemble, and minimization over a randomly selected target subset. The paper tests continuous-control benchmarks; it does not establish that ensemble disagreement is calibrated uncertainty or that high UTD solves missing reward coverage. Stage should not adopt a large UTD before a bounded value/target audit. [Chen et al., ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.05982), [author repository](https://github.com/watchernyu/REDQ).

### DDPGfD, TD3fD and DQfD provenance

DDPGfD supplies the primary continuous-action demonstration paper: demonstration and agent transitions share replay; prioritized sampling, one/n-step critic losses, repeated updates and regularization propagate sparse rewards. It explicitly warns that stale replay and unseen states can cause erroneous Q values and divergence. It is not an episode-success filter: a failed rollout remains legitimate RL data if its actual transitions and rewards are preserved. [Vecerik et al., 2017/2018, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.08817).

Acme lists TD3fD but leaves its paper field blank. Its actual JAX implementation wraps the existing TD3 builder with the generic LfD builder, inserting external demonstrations into replay. This review found an official implementation, **not a verified standalone canonical TD3fD paper**. Demonstration insertion ratio is not the same thing as RLPD's minibatch sampling ratio. [Acme agent list](https://dm-acme.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/agents.html), [TD3fD wrapper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/acme/master/acme/agents/jax/lfd/td3fd.py), [generic builder](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/acme/master/acme/agents/jax/lfd/builder.py), [configuration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/acme/master/acme/agents/jax/lfd/config.py).

DQfD pretrains on demonstrations, retains them permanently and combines TD, n-step, large-margin supervised and regularization losses. Later agent data can improve beyond the demonstrator; the paper reports this in some Atari games. It does not require the demonstrator to label every later state, but its action-margin term and DQN action selection are discrete. [Hester et al., AAAI 2018, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.03732).

### JSRL changes which side of the handover is trusted

JSRL first executes a guide prefix and then lets the learner improve the suffix, progressively reducing guide duration. The guide can be suboptimal; it must bring the learner into useful states, rather than solve every state caused by the learner. This reverses the recent student-prefix/teacher-recovery test. The paper also shows that a good initial Actor plus fresh untrained Q can forget, so naïvely initializing a new critic is not enough. Its sample-complexity results depend on stated assumptions and are not a car-navigation guarantee. [Uchendu et al., ICML 2023, §§3–4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/uchendu23a/uchendu23a.pdf), [author project](https://jumpstart-rl.github.io/).

A Stage adaptation should prefer a verified successful guide prefix, reconstruct the student's own memory from all actual observations and executed actions, preserve elapsed clock/actuator state, and learn from its actual suffix outcomes. A recurrent guide's hidden state must never be copied into the learner. Train-time switching requires a separately registered protocol; final deployment and acceptance use the learner alone from the original start. This is an adaptation of JSRL, not the original paper's verified recurrent implementation.

### SIL is an addition, not a substitute for recovery coverage

SIL uses the positive part of R−V(s) to weight off-policy action imitation and value fitting. Thus an unsuccessful episode can contribute if it beats the baseline; terminal GOAL is not intrinsic to the formula. The paper's Venture failure is a useful counterexample: no rewarding experience meant no good experience to exploit. In Stage, preserve explicit safe-GOAL metadata separately from return advantage; do not reinterpret high shaped return or a 0.2145 m miss as successful recovery. [Oh et al., ICML 2018, §§2 and 4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/oh18b/oh18b.pdf), [author repository](https://github.com/junhyukoh/self-imitation-learning).

### Recurrent replay must preserve the causal history

R2D3 learns from separate demonstration/agent buffers with n-step double Q-learning. It uses overlapping length-80 sequences, an initial 40-step burn-in, and losses on the remaining portion; episodes are not crossed. The paper calls burn-in an approximation to replaying from episode start and notes that the most memory-demanding tasks still fail. Its small optimal demo ratios were task-specific findings, not a universal reason to choose 1/256 for Stage. [Paine/Gulcehre et al., ICLR 2020, §§2, 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.01387).

The official Acme R2D2 learner reconstructs recurrent state before the loss and removes warmup steps from training. Its R2D3 implementation mixes demo/agent datasets and invokes that learner. Stored hidden states can be stale; arbitrary zero state at a late fragment and teacher hidden copied into a student are different errors. For Stage's small retained corpus, a full causal-prefix reconstruction is the stronger audit reference. For any truncated implementation, compare outputs and targets against that reference before claiming equivalence. [R2D2 learner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/acme/master/acme/agents/tf/r2d2/learning.py), [R2D3 agent](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/acme/master/acme/agents/tf/r2d3/agent.py). The R2D2 paper's OpenReview full text was browser-challenged during this review; its burn-in implementation was inspected directly rather than claiming successful full-paper access.

Ni et al. provide a directly relevant recurrent continuous-control baseline: implementation choices, separate actor/critic recurrence and task-appropriate context substantially affect results. Their empirical comparisons support investigating a careful baseline before a specialized memory invention; they do not prove Stage's current memory is sufficient. [ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/ni22a.html), [author discussion](https://blog.ml.cmu.edu/2022/08/26/recurrent-model-free-rl-can-be-a-strong-baseline-for-many-pomdps-2/), [official code](https://github.com/twni2016/pomdp-baselines).

## Why a successful teacher can fail to recover

DAgger addresses observations induced by the learner, rather than only expert-trajectory observations. Its theoretical comparison is relative to the expert; a bounded expert cost-to-go disadvantage/recovery condition is important in translating action error into task cost. It does **not** assert that every queried teacher rollout must succeed, nor that cloning an unsuccessful teacher creates success. Worst-case compounding motivates induced-state data; it is not a measured T² drift law for this continuous car. [Ross et al., AISTATS 2011, §§2–3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a/ross11a.pdf).

Stage's existing “successful suffix only” rule is stricter than that algorithmic definition and must remain intact unless explicitly changed. T611/T617 failed teacher transitions can enter an RL buffer with their real outcomes, but cannot silently enter the successful-BC set. A mixed teacher takeover is off-policy data; matching behavior log-probability or restoring recurrent memory does not turn it into on-policy student data.

## Concrete recommendation for the next proposal, not an authorized run

1. **Resolve target geometry and action authority first using saved data.** Reconstruct closest-approach target bearing in the car frame, heading/yaw rate, longitudinal/lateral velocity, wheel/actuator state, actual mapped steering, raw clipping and drive around the miss; compare retained T608's successful terminal segment with rejected candidates. Confirm the native GOAL event, goal reference point and time-limit handling. Distinguish “could not turn tightly enough on this pass” from “could turn but issued the wrong command.” If native continuous traces are absent, report that gap; do not infer them from decision records.
2. **Design one bounded recurrent off-policy comparison.** Keep the retained Actor architecture, causal sensor/goal inputs and direct action map; use separate training critics and target networks. Preserve protected prior successes and actual failure transitions, with immutable task/source/outcome ownership. Use an RLPD-style fixed prior/online sampling baseline, successful-prefix JSRL-style roll-in only during training, and a simpler TD3+prior-replay reference under matched interaction and optimizer budgets. Within live data, explicitly balance old tasks and new difficult tasks: static historical action matching cannot replace new closed-loop old-task coverage. Count all guide-prefix, learner-suffix, replay and acceptance costs separately. An RNN adaptation, warm start, combined curriculum and existing retention constraints must be declared rather than called the original algorithms.
3. **Gate Actor learning on action-sensitive value evidence.** Check native terminal versus time-limit bootstrap masks, actual held-action duration and physical-time discounting, correct executed actions in replay, full-prefix recurrent targets, and held-out task/trajectory splits. Evaluate Q ranking against real recorded outcome returns where coverage exists; an ensemble agreeing off support is not proof. Do not train a risk-blind “progress value” and call it safe arrival probability.
4. **Retain by closed-loop task outcomes.** Keep the original 34 IDs and recovery checks, and report new successes separately from recovered historical successes. Protected replay and pointwise function regularization can reduce forgetting pressure but do not certify trajectory preservation. Failed candidates remain evidence; no repeated pulse grid or same-mechanism restart follows from this note.

The 0.2 m criterion must stay unchanged. A closest recorded point still 1.4–2.1 cm outside that boundary remains failure; the +pulse improves the recorded minimum by only about 6.1 mm. If a future curriculum uses near-goal starts, report full-origin success separately; it cannot prove the preceding long-horizon approach policy.

HER is a later optional data-efficiency variable, not part of the first comparison. It can relabel failed trajectories with achieved goals for off-policy learning, but Stage must first demonstrate a complete goal-conditioned reward/terminal/history contract and distinguish relabelled success from the requested original goal. It cannot turn a miss of the original 0.2 m target into success. [Original HER paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495), [official implementation](https://github.com/openai/baselines/blob/master/baselines/her/README.md).

Training-only privileged critics (asymmetric/centralized training) or model-based recovery teachers are **hypothetical scope changes**, not implied by this recommendation. They could still distill into one causal Actor at deployment, but require explicit approval of training information, label-generation cost and independent student validation. They do not justify importing Phase planning evidence or deploying a planner/teacher/router. No reviewed source guarantees that any of these methods solves the present car geometry, terminal tolerance, missing coverage, or strict retention constraints.
