## Sources, exact reading scope, and limits

The sources below were opened through primary publication/author pages on the review date. PDF text was inspected selectively; this is **not** a claim to have read every page, supplement, repository, or all relevant recent literature. Search results and abstracts were used for discovery, not substituted for the specified methods sections.

1. **Schulman et al., PPO, arXiv v2 (28 August 2017).** Read §2.1, §2.2, §3 Eq. (7), and §5 Algorithm 1 excerpts. PPO alternates behavior-policy collection and repeated surrogate optimization; clipping suppresses an incentive to move sampled ratios further in selected directions. This supports a bounded clipped update using recorded behavior densities. The paper's actor-critic presentation includes value estimation; T506's peer-return advantage and frozen Value are adaptations, so T506 is not an exact reproduction. A lower bound on the unclipped surrogate is not a certified lower bound on every native task's success probability. [Original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347).

2. **Florensa et al., Goal GAN, ICML 2018 / PMLR 80.** Read §3.2–3.3, §4.1 Eq. (4), §4.3 algorithm discussion, and §5 baseline/setup excerpts. Goals of intermediate difficulty are identified through policy success probability, with assumptions about interpolation and transfer to neighboring goals. Experiments use goal-conditioned policies and TRPO. This supports concentrating training near a demonstrated capability frontier in principle. T506 measures neither intermediate success probability nor those transfer assumptions and trains no GAN. The paper also motivates an L2-distance baseline through its susceptibility to local minima; this cautions against equating geometric closeness with useful progress, but does not directly refute T506's sampling heuristic. [Original PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/florensa18a/florensa18a.pdf).

3. **Portelas et al., ALP-GMM, CoRL 2019 / PMLR 100 (2020 proceedings).** Read §4.1, nearest-neighbor ALP Eq. (2), and §5 evaluation/§5.1 comparison excerpts. ALP compares episodic reward with a historical neighboring task parameter, fits a GMM over parameter/ALP pairs, and samples regions with high absolute progress while retaining exploration. It can revisit declining competence. Evidence is conditional: in the quadrupedal Stump Tracks condition random sampling also rapidly masters nearly all test tasks; ALP-GMM has no universal advantage. The authors use SAC walkers and parameterized environments, rather than this vehicle and fixed three goals. T506 has no temporal progress estimate, GMM, adaptive sampler, or competence model. [Original PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v100/portelas20a/portelas20a.pdf).

4. **Ahmadian et al., RLOO, ACL 2024.** Read §2.3 including its estimator and the explicit iid same-prompt sampling condition; also read the introduction's sequence-level setup excerpts. RLOO subtracts the other samples' mean return without fitting a critic. This motivates a peer-only baseline. Its demonstrations concern sequence-level RLHF and do not establish a physical-time robot RTG estimator, navigation benefit, or collision safety. T506's time alignment, RMS scaling, clipping, retention loss, and eligibility rules need their own accounting. [Original PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.662.pdf).

5. **Wang, He and Tan, Truly Proximal Policy Optimization, UAI 2019 / PMLR 115 (2020 proceedings).** Read abstract and introduction pp. 1–2, rather than claiming a full proof/implementation review. The authors explicitly challenge PPO's ability to strictly bound ratios or enforce a trust region. This is primary-source counterevidence to treating clipping as a retention/safety certificate. Their proposed rollback loss is a different algorithm; T506's full Actor/Adam restoration after measured failure is an engineering acceptance rule, not an implementation of TR-PPO-RB or its theorem. [Original PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v115/wang20b/wang20b.pdf).

Author implementation checks were limited to the accessible [Goal GAN author project page](https://sites.google.com/view/goalgeneration4rl), its linked [rllab-curriculum README](https://github.com/florensacc/rllab-curriculum), the [ALP-GMM author repository README](https://github.com/flowersteam/teachDeepRL), and [OpenAI Baselines PPO2 `model.py`](https://github.com/openai/baselines/blob/master/baselines/ppo2/model.py) advantage-normalization excerpt. The ALP-GMM README identifies the SAC student and lists ALP-GMM/RIAC/Oracle/Random/Covar-GMM controls. Baselines subtracts mean and divides by standard deviation; this is not evidence that within-group RMS scaling is unbiased. These are mutable `master` views, not pinned or executed reproductions. No author code is copied or installed, and no code/license compatibility conclusion is needed for this documentation-only review.

## Peer baseline: conditional independence has a narrow scope

For each fixed task, collect four complete fresh trajectories from the same frozen behavior policy with independent iid exploration, sigma 0.2. For member i at physical elapsed time t, a proposed baseline is the mean of the **other three** trajectories' return-to-go aligned at that physical time. The raw advantage is its own RTG minus that peer mean. Timestamps, RTG discount units, alignment/interpolation, terminal continuation, collector truncation and missing-data handling must follow one registered rule. Do not use decision-row index as a substitute for physical elapsed time or average only whichever peers are still alive at a chosen index.

The useful independence argument is conditional on a fixed task and fixed behavior policy: independent peer noise makes a peer-only return independent of member i's current action draw, conditional on i's preceding history and elapsed time. Subtracting such an action-independent baseline has zero expected score contribution in the ordinary unconditioned on-policy score estimator. This is an analytical application of the RLOO idea, not a theorem proved for T506 by the RLHF paper. It does not require peer states to equal i's current state, and it does not establish that the baseline reduces variance.

Several changes prevent calling the **complete update** unbiased:

- A group RMS computed using all four trajectories depends on member i's own outcome. Dividing by that random scale is not covered by the peer-only independence argument, even when gradients through the scale are stopped.
- PPO clipping and repeated finite-batch optimization target a surrogate; later optimizer steps are not fresh on-policy score estimates.
- Any eligibility condition based on seeing a safe goal in the collected batch conditions on outcomes. Omitting failed members, choosing successful subsets, retrying seeds until a success appears, or recomputing peers after filtering would further alter the estimator.
- Retention on stored success histories is an additional objective. Those histories are not newly sampled on-policy PPO data and must not be counted as fresh exploration or assigned current behavior likelihoods.

Use the registered physical-time discount weight (the protocol's gamma-to-elapsed-time factor) explicitly with the RTG surrogate; do not silently mix gamma per decision, per physical step, and per second. Neither the RLOO source nor PPO establishes T506's particular discount/time convention. Keep Value frozen with **zero Value optimizer steps**. Report the whole method as critic-free PPO-style fine-tuning with a three-peer physical-time RTG baseline and group RMS normalization, without an unbiasedness claim.

## Bounded proposal and what would count as evidence

The proposed T506 protocol fixes these three tasks before collection, four fresh own-policy iid sigma-0.2 trajectories each: **12 fresh training trajectories plus 12 exact reconstructions**, not 24 independent samples. At most **16 live Actor updates**; any shadow/audit optimizer reconstruction is counted separately. Keep the actual accepted T505 old8 and seven field success histories as retention data. No SIL/new teacher/planner/reward modification is introduced. Four trajectories per task are not four independent training-seed replications.

After the proposed update, check the same candidate on **old8 plus the seven protected field tasks: 15 native cases plus 15 exact replays**. Any loss of a protected task triggers full Actor **and Adam** rollback and stops this proposal. Only after passing that gate run the other **17 field native cases plus the specified 24 field exact replays**. The seven guard field outcomes are reused from that same unchanged policy in the field24 total, not represented as newly collected independent native outcomes. Keep the separately required replay counts explicit even where they concern overlapping IDs.

Research acceptance requires all original seven field success **IDs** retained, all old8 safe, **more than seven** field-qualified successes out of 24, and **at most ten** field contacts; otherwise restore the complete parent Actor/Adam. Missing/invalid/censored checks are not passes, and native success and field-qualified success must remain distinct where the original contract distinguishes them. Exact replay tests reproducibility; it does not add independent generalization evidence. The hard gate measures the original finite-task predicate for the tested candidate, not every intermediate optimizer state or every possible rollout.

The three tasks were chosen using T505 failures, and the seven retained field cases influence acceptance: this is a development curriculum/pilot. Nearest-distance ranking is **not ALP**, a success-probability competence estimate, a learned dynamics/value model, a reachability proof, or a safety certificate. Distances omit heading, speed, obstacle topology and the ability to remain in the goal region. New native successes would support local improvement on this finite panel; failure would reject this fixed budget and configuration, not demonstrate that PPO, goal curricula, the vehicle, or the field are universally unlearnable. No outcome establishes unseen-start/cross-map robustness, global safety, monotonic improvement or algorithmic originality.
