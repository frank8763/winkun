Support and counterevidence:

- [Geiger et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/4f5c422f4d49a5a807eda27434231040-Paper.pdf), viewed pp.1–4, distinguish information present in representations from intervention-tested causal effects. Their arithmetic example and formal alignment motivate explicit counterfactual hypotheses. However, their interventions replace activations across inputs in arithmetic/NLI models. T682 replaces parameters in a recurrent vehicle controller; it does not establish their formal causal-abstraction theorem or a human-interpretable navigation mechanism.
- [Morcos et al., ICLR 2018](https://openreview.net/references/pdf?id=ryIaiATBz), viewed pp.1–3 and 7–8, use controlled activation ablations and show that selectivity poorly predicts functional importance. Their finding that mean versus zero replacement changes damage is a warning that conclusions depend on the intervention baseline. These image-classification activation experiments do not validate weight replacement, policy retention, or safe control. T501 replacement must be named explicitly rather than described as removing an intrinsically independent skill.
- [Salimans et al., arXiv:1703.03864v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03864v2), viewed pp.1–3, optimize a Gaussian-smoothed population objective by episode-level parameter perturbation. Individual successful variants do not establish an improved center or a reliable gradient. The paper also reports brittleness without reparameterization. Its MuJoCo/Atari scaling results cannot override T680 or authorize more seeds, directions, or scales.
