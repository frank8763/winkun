## Policy Distillation

**Actual scope read:** Rusu et al., arXiv:1511.06295v2, §3.1–3.3; adjacent §2 discussion of teacher/student trajectory collection. §3 transfers teacher supervision to a student using teacher-generated observation/Q-vector pairs. It compares best-action NLL, Q-vector MSE, and KL between temperature-transformed action scores. The demonstrated formulation uses discrete Atari actions; its Q softmax can sharpen targets, rather than always soften them. §3.3 also uses task-specific output controllers selected by task ID. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.06295#S3)

**T559 interpretation:** transferring successful control into another network is established policy-distillation practice. Continuous location/command regression through the existing command clipping is an engineering adaptation, not the original discrete-Q objective. A single shared readout without task-ID routing also differs from the paper's multi-game controllers. No temperature-softmax loss or guaranteed transfer success follows from this citation. Replacing compact RBF corrections while freezing the backbone changes representation and trainable capacity together, so the pilot cannot isolate width or smoothness as the cause of any result. [Primary basis](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.06295#S3)

## Kickstarting Deep Reinforcement Learning

**Actual scope read:** Schmitt et al., arXiv:1803.03835v1, abstract, §1, §2.1–2.3. Kickstarting combines a student RL objective with teacher-policy cross-entropy on trajectories generated by the student; the teacher weight changes during training. Teacher and student architectures may differ. Population Based Training is the paper's mechanism for adapting training hyperparameters. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.03835#S2)

**T559 interpretation:** this supports teacher-assisted architectural transfer as a precedent, but fixed offline fitting of saved success histories has neither its student-distribution teacher queries nor its RL objective/PBT. It is not a Kickstarting reproduction. A later teacher-assisted RL experiment would require its own bounded protocol and evidence that the teacher supplies useful recovery actions on student-visited states. [Primary basis](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.03835#S2)

## DART: Noise Injection for Robust Imitation Learning

**Actual scope read:** Laskey et al., original CoRL/PMLR 78 paper, abstract, §1, §3, §4–4.1 and the §4.2 proposition/qualification paragraph; not the appendix proof or a full experimental replication. DART perturbs the supervisor's control stream while collecting demonstrations, obtains the supervisor's corrective actions, and iteratively estimates/scales noise using learner error. §3 distinguishes supervised loss under the supervisor distribution from loss under the learner's induced trajectory distribution. [Primary PDF, pp. 1–6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/laskey17a/laskey17a.pdf)

**T559 interpretation:** four fixed, physically executed successful perturbation histories provide sparse recovery examples; they do not implement DART's adaptive noise procedure or establish recovery coverage. Excluding imposed pulse commands from autonomous-action labels is a project data contract: imposed disturbances are not the supervisor's intended corrective commands. Independently perturbing observation columns would not reproduce the paper's control-stream intervention. [Primary basis](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v78/laskey17a/laskey17a.pdf)

## Bounded conclusion for this pilot

The proposed training support is only the same T550 23 successful development histories plus four actual safe perturbation histories, with forced-pulse labels excluded; no new acquisition goals are included. Offline action/location fit measures agreement on that finite support. It cannot establish autonomous GOAL, closed-loop stability, recovery outside that support, held-out-layout generalization, or real-car transfer. Smoothness alone supplies no such guarantee. These are project-level limits inferred from the distribution distinction above and the local [T558 findings/next plan](../NEXT_AFTER_T557_AND_T558.md), not new theorems from the cited papers. Native qualification remains necessary after the fixed offline gate. T550 has weak or absent validated teacher evidence outside this tiny support; its arbitrary output on a student deviation must not be treated as a verified recovery expert. The pilot is standard supervised policy transfer with a project-specific representation change, not an original algorithm claim.
