## Primary methods and their actual promises

### PCGrad — NeurIPS 2020

PCGrad forms separate task-loss gradients; if a pair has negative dot product it removes the conflicting component, using randomized task order for multiple tasks, then combines gradients. It addresses local gradient interference, not missing successful experience. The basic two-task theorem assumes convex differentiable losses, Lipschitz total gradient and step size at most inverse smoothness; convergence may stop where gradients are exactly opposite, rather than at the joint optimum. Its one-step superiority result additionally requires particular conflict, magnitude, curvature and step-size conditions. Empirical RL results do not establish monotonic navigation success. Applying surgery before Adam is supported as an implementation pattern, not a certificate that Adam's realized displacement preserves every task. [Paper, algorithm and theory](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.06782v3), [authors' code](https://github.com/tianheyu927/PCGrad).

**Stage inference:** separate fresh LEFT/RIGHT gradients would make surgery definable, but choosing task partitions/normalization changes the objective. A negative cosine on four trajectories can reflect stochastic estimation or different state visitation. A positive cosine does not rule out nonlinear closed-loop regressions. Stage already used PCGrad before T287; this is not an original proposal or an untried remedy. Sparse Jacobian projection also failed in T285 despite very small selected-state action errors: [historical result](../long_distance_20261101/T285_FINDINGS.md).

### CAGrad — NeurIPS 2021

For loss gradients gi and average g0, CAGrad maximizes the worst local improvement min_i gi·d subject to ||d−g0||≤c||g0||. The update is θ←θ−αd. Its theorem assumes differentiable losses with H-Lipschitz gradients, lower-bounded average loss and fixed 0<α≤1/H. For 0≤c<1 it bounds the sum of squared average-gradient norms and reaches average-loss stationarity; this is not a global-optimum guarantee for neural networks. Larger c changes the guarantee toward Pareto stationarity. The minimum local improvement can still be negative: CAGrad is not a hard no-regression constraint. The authors' toy implementation passes the combined gradient to Adam, which demonstrates compatibility, not transfer of the deterministic-GD theorem to mature Adam/PPO. [Paper, especially §3 and theorem3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.14048), [official implementation](https://github.com/Cranial-XIX/CAGrad), [toy solver and Adam use](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Cranial-XIX/CAGrad/main/toy.py).

**Stage inference:** CAGrad could address competing useful LEFT/RIGHT update directions. It cannot manufacture LEFT8 successful-state coverage, solve partial observability by itself, or ensure preservation of 335 successful closed-loop trajectories. Loss scales and whether old retention is a third objective must be fixed before interpreting any comparison.

### CLEAR — NeurIPS 2019, not ICML 2019

CLEAR mixes new and replayed experience in actor-critic training. It uses V-trace truncated importance ratios for off-policy RL, adds replay-only historical-policy-to-current-policy KL and historical-value squared-error cloning, and typically mixes new/replay experience 50/50. It does not require task boundaries. Its experiments distinguish simultaneous task interference from sequential forgetting. The authors explicitly describe replay/off-policy correction as complementary to cloning; historical data are not fresh on-policy samples. Results on Atari/DMLab are empirical retention evidence, not per-task safety guarantees. [Original paper, §3–5](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/fa7cdfad1a5aaf8370ebeda47a1ff1c3-Paper.pdf).

**Stage inference:** the useful immediate idea is representative own-experience coverage plus distribution-aware retention. CLEAR does not prescribe balanced LEFT/RIGHT strata; that is our controlled adaptation. A function MSE against the initialization on a few histories is only one retention component, not CLEAR. Exact recurrent histories, behavior distributions and value targets matter. Do not label deterministic successful evaluation actions as stochastic behavior-policy samples or insert them into ordinary current-PPO advantage updates.

### A-GEM / GPM: useful cautions, lower priority

A-GEM projects a current gradient against an averaged episodic-memory reference gradient when their dot product is negative. An average-memory condition can hide individual task regressions; its supervised setting does not validate off-policy PPO replay. [Authors' paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00420), [official implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/agem).

GPM constructs stored activation subspaces using SVD and projects subsequent gradients away from important old subspaces. Its primary evidence is image classification; applying it to shared recurrent weights and full trajectory gradients needs new assumptions. It is not a ready-made closed-loop preservation guarantee. [Authors' paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09762).

## Two important mathematical traps

**Zero initial preservation gradient.** For fixed reference histories h and f0=fθ0, Lret=mean||fθ(h)−f0(h)||² has gradient zero at θ0, even if its Hessian is substantial. KL cloning of an identical distribution likewise starts at a minimum. Consequently adding an average retention gradient as a PCGrad/CAGrad/A-GEM task can provide no initial constraint. Later it penalizes drift only on its sampled histories. This statement follows directly by differentiating the loss, not from a paper's safety theorem. Full-history BPTT must recompute each candidate's hidden states; stale hidden vectors do not represent the same function constraint.

**Mature Adam changes the direction.** Adam's update uses accumulated first and second moments, approximately Δθ=−α mhat/(sqrt(vhat)+ε), rather than −αd. Thus gi·d≥0 need not imply gi·Δθ≤0. Projecting raw gradients, then applying existing moments can undo the intended geometry. Measure each task's first-order loss change against the *actual complete optimizer displacement* and compare finite loss changes; retain the moments in every clone and rollback. Resetting Adam or projecting final displacements would introduce additional interventions, not establish ordinary CAGrad's guarantee. This is a deduction from [Adam's update rule](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6980), not evidence that Adam caused this run's failures.

Finite smooth-loss improvement also does not ensure GOAL/contact preservation: parameter changes alter actions, future observations and recurrent states, potentially amplifying small errors. T284/285 are Stage counterexamples to overinterpreting sparse local anchors. Full383 testing is a development-set acceptance filter, not unseen-state safety or generalization.

## One next controlled experiment to consider after T478 ends

**A single bounded paired continuation with matched data, differing only in own-success retention coverage.** This is a recommendation requiring a separately reviewed future registration, not a protocol or authorization to launch.

Start both arms from the same retained Actor/V/two-Adam checkpoint after T478 is finished and audited. Hold architecture, PPO objective, fresh collection tasks/seeds, pooled advantages, update count, optimizer state and total reference presentation/weight fixed. Use the same fresh batch for the paired local comparison so the initial policy data are valid for both; do not keep reusing that batch across an unlimited sequence of changed policies. Control uses T478's reference selection. Treatment uses complete already verified own histories covering every existing eight-success ID (five LEFT/three RIGHT at the present retained baseline), with equal total LEFT/RIGHT reference weight and fixed old/six coverage. Fix per-history normalization so longer RIGHT histories cannot silently dominate. Newly acquired valid own successes can enter only at an explicitly defined later boundary. No external demonstrations or runtime teacher.

For the immediate coverage contrast, preserve own-policy distributions/functions on those histories; do not simultaneously switch PPO to V-trace, add a value-cloning objective or introduce gradient surgery. Call it CLEAR-inspired retention coverage, not full CLEAR. Full CLEAR-style replay RL is a separate larger intervention needing stored behavior densities, recurrent-history reconstruction and audited off-policy correction. The deterministic evaluation histories can support function retention; they do not automatically support replay RL.

Record actual LEFT/RIGHT PPO gradients separately from retention gradients, their norms/dot products, and their directional loss changes under mature-Adam displacement at the shared start and across the fixed update schedule. Use these as explanatory diagnostics, not an adaptive trigger to change the run. Preserve every currently retained success ID (335 at the stated baseline, or the exact audited successor set), require a new eight success, and test full383 before any retained update; count gains/losses by side and distance, with whole-state rollback on regression. Hold an independent assessment set out of optimization if later generalization claims are wanted.

**Interpretation:** better retention with matched update/data presentation supports inadequate reference coverage as a practical contributor. Persistent negative actual LEFT/RIGHT directional effects after coverage matching would justify a later CAGrad comparison. If gradients favor both sides yet closed-loop results regress, focus next on coverage of the changed trajectory distribution, estimator error, finite-step nonlinearity or representation limits; the result does not isolate which one. A finite failed paired experiment cannot prove capacity insufficiency or that the navigation task is impossible.

Do not repeat a plain head-only intervention: T455 already froze 52,736 encoder/core parameters and trained the130-parameter head, ending six47/54 and old198/275. T454's larger encoder/GRU drift on fixed histories was descriptive, not a closed-loop causal attribution. T447 found the old LEFT5 BC gradient locally aligned with the joint direction (cosine +.236668), including a favorable mature-Adam directional estimate; that historical BC result cannot establish current fresh PPO LEFT/RIGHT conflict.
