## Prioritized explanations, all falsifiable

1. **Induced-state recovery coverage is insufficient.** Prediction: small actual learner deviations lead to actions that differ strongly from performance on demonstrated histories; both existing read-only tests and centre-vs-neighbour outcomes exhibit this. More varied *valid* recovery data or better closed-loop sequence policy should improve autonomous nearby resets. The current two successful corrections do not label all learner states.
2. **Single-current-action imitation under-specifies driving timing.** Current recurrent network has memory, but each supervised target is one current drive/steer vector, not an explicitly predicted short future command sequence. Hypothesis: future-action supervision can reduce timing/coordination errors. It must be tested; the present data do not prove that action chunks are necessary or sufficient.
3. **New/old losses and closed-loop retention conflict.** Actual rollback and wide regression are evidence of interference; simultaneous complete-success-history rehearsal is an existing partial response. A new representation must retain old/narrow successful behaviour in physical tests, not only reduce reference MSE. No claim that all old failures originated in the latest fit.
4. **Observation/representation limits are possible but unproven.** True GRU16 carry and measured steering angle/rate are already present. Selected raw histories are distinguishable. Additional memory/camera should follow a measurable information deficit, rather than being claimed as an automatic cure.

## Preferred next mechanism after T368 closes

Prepare a small **short-action-sequence imitation proposal plus factual-history rehearsal**, followed by separately budgeted RL only if autonomous control improves. Reuse existing verified human and autonomous successful histories; do not require another human session to begin a zero-step engineering/data contract. This changes the learning target, not the environment, deployment route or navigation acceptance.

Proposed first-stage design (not preregistered/implemented yet): same causal120input/recurrent state; a head predicts five drive/steer commands covering1s at the existing0.2sdecision spacing. At runtime recompute every0.2s from actual sensors and execute only the first command, so it does not blindly execute a1sopen-loop tape. Predict future expert actions as supervision, never provide future observations to the Actor. Future-target/end-of-episode masks must respect true terminal boundaries; T364first20learner actions receive zero human-target weight wherever they appear, while factual full history remains unrolled. Keep data source/weights explicit and compare capacity (a five-step/ten-output replacement of the existing two-output Linear head would add136parameters, not require a large Transformer). Cloning/repeating the parent current-action head can establish exact initial deployment equivalence, but is not proof of trained benefit.

This is action-chunk-supervision inspired by ACT, not full ACT: no automatic claim of Transformer/CVAE, ACT temporal ensembling, multimodal coverage, or a demonstrated theorem. Executing only the first head at every existing decision does not reduce the number of closed-loop decision steps; it is multi-horizon supervised prediction, not ACT's temporal-ensemble controller or a demonstrated effective-horizon reduction. The receding first-action-only variant's benefit is a hypothesis; its horizon and capacity must be locked once in a separate bounded protocol, with the single-action parent as a fixed control. Do not open a horizon/seed/loss grid. A sequence head can still average incompatible modes or extrapolate poorly; limited recovery-data coverage remains a hard limitation. Rehearsal on complete actual five-wide and two-narrow histories, original old references, and native success-ID retention remains necessary to assess regressions.

## Primary-source context

- [Ross, Gordon & Bagnell2011, DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html): actions affect the later observation distribution; successful behaviour cloning on expert observations need not remain reliable on learner-induced states. Full DAgger requires expert labels on the relevant learner distribution. Our two handoffs are a limited correction collection, not full DAgger or its guarantees.
- [Laskey et al.2017, DART](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09327): disturbances during *expert demonstration* elicit recovery actions. Adding correlated noise to the learner in T368is not DART and cannot produce expert recovery labels without an expert.
- [Zhao et al.,RSS2023, ACT](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss19/p016.html): action chunking was proposed to reduce effective horizons and error accumulation in demonstrated manipulation. Its reported manipulation success and data quantity do not guarantee navigation performance; transferring the idea to our small causal lidar policy is a new experiment, not established benefit or originality.

Use the companion primary-source review for exact ACT/temporal-ensemble caveats. This proposal combines established ideas; journal novelty remains unestablished and requires controlled ablations/generalization/safety evidence after basic navigation succeeds.
