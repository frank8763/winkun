# T357: passage feasibility and policy approach are separate problems

The frozen T353 iid Actor was run from the original six-metre starting snapshots. In the new, physically passable 0.70 m scene with an 80 s course cap, all five consumed LEFT resets collided (44.872–50.212 s). The 1.17 m / 80 s historical centre succeeded at 56.006 s. The unmodified 0.57 m / 56 s benchmark centres reproduced LEFT collision and RIGHT timeout. Eight trajectories and eight exact native regenerations used 19 commands including three zero-physics geometry environments, 393,942 native steps, and 127.273221 s. Zero Actor/optimizer updates. Independent audit PASS, including complete 120/history/hidden/reward reconstruction and explicit historical overlap.

The new narrow centre approaches at x≈0.758 m, y≈−1.929 m, yaw≈−1.317 rad at 40 s; it collides at x≈0.906 m, y≈−2.424 m. T356's three verified local straight passages use x≈0.43 m and yaw≈−π/2. These different initial configurations show that successful local clearance alone is insufficient: the existing wide-trained policy approaches the narrow opening on an unsuitable lateral line and heading. This is a trajectory observation, not a proof that a single action or algorithm component causes the failure.

T355 never trained the smaller openings; repeating its wide-stage rollout would not supply narrow-task experience. The next bounded learning pilot should train directly on the separately identified feasible 0.70 m scene from the real origin. Keep wide-scene and old-task checks, all rejections, and the untouched T255 safety baseline. Do not claim local passage success, wider-course success, or five consumed development cases establish original six-metre stability.
